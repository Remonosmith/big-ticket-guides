Peak Saunas Outdoor Sauna Reviews and Comparison: Models, Prices, and What to Check

Peak Saunas sells outdoor saunas that are designed to be installed as permanent backyard structures. Unlike small indoor units, these are larger, higher-priced systems that require space, utilities, and planning.

This page compares the company's published outdoor models, their prices, and the checks a buyer should complete before ordering.

Prices checked November 10, 2026.

What is Peak Saunas?

Peak Saunas is an outdoor-sauna manufacturer that sells wood-burning and hybrid electric/wood systems.

Its published catalog includes several barrel and cabin-style models in different sizes.

The company publishes specifications, brochures, and installation information for each model. It also describes financing options on its website.

Available options, dimensions, materials, and capacities vary by model.

Who is a Peak Saunas outdoor sauna for?

These saunas are intended for homeowners with:

  • Outdoor space for a freestanding sauna.
  • Sufficient budget for installation and utilities.
  • Time for delivery, assembly, and site preparation.

They are not entry-level plug-and-play saunas. Buyers looking for a compact indoor model or a low-cost option should compare alternatives first.

Kilimanjaro: The higher-capacity choice

The Kilimanjaro seats six people and is listed at $14,450.

It is the largest model in Peak Saunas' published catalog, although other configurations may be offered.

Its advertised specifications describe a wood-burning and hybrid electric option.

Buyers should confirm the exact dimensions, foundation requirements, and heater configuration.

El Capitan: The mid-sized cabin option

The El Capitan seats four people and is listed at $16,750.

It is slightly more expensive than the Kilimanjaro despite seating fewer people, which may reflect different materials, dimensions, or configuration options.

The product page lists a wood-burning and hybrid electric heat source.

Published information includes a 5.6 kW electric heater option and an overall footprint of approximately 90 inches wide, 75 inches deep, and 96 inches high.

Matterhorn: The smallest outdoor option

The Matterhorn is the smallest model in this comparison.

It seats two people and is listed at $10,450.

It uses a wood-burning or hybrid electric heat source and an overall footprint of approximately 90 inches wide, 63 inches deep, and 96 inches high.

It is the most affordable model in the published catalog, although its smaller capacity may not suit families.

Installation and utilities

Outdoor saunas require preparation beyond the advertised price.

Buyers should confirm:

  • Delivery access and unloading requirements.
  • Foundation and site preparation.
  • Electrical service capacity and wiring costs.
  • Wood storage and ventilation requirements.
  • Local building permits.
  • Weather protection and insulation.

The company publishes site-preparation guidance, but the owner is responsible for confirming local requirements.

What to check before buying

  1. Confirm the exact model, heater type, and included accessories.
  2. Compare the total cost, including delivery, installation, and utilities.
  3. Obtain written lead-time and delivery estimates.
  4. Confirm warranty terms, including coverage period and exclusions.
  5. Review the deposit, cancellation, and refund terms.
  6. Confirm the dimensions and door clearances.
  7. Check whether the advertised price includes installation.
  8. Ask about available financing options.
  9. Confirm service and parts availability.
  10. Determine whether the sauna affects home insurance or property taxes.
